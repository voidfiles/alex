Every relationship must include its own logic object. Do not assign a single
quantifier to a relation type: different assertions of the same predicate may
have different scope. Choose the weakest faithful logical reading.

subject_quantifier:
- all: The source supports EVERY member of the source class satisfying the
  stated restriction. A generic tendency or one example does not justify all.
- some: The source establishes at least one member of the source class with
  the stated restriction. This is not a restriction on every class member.
- individual: The source endpoint is a particular named instance.
- unspecified: The source does not establish one of the above scopes.

object_quantifier (only for ordinary properties):
- some: At least one relation target belongs to the target CLASS. This asserts
  existence of a target, not merely that the relationship is plausible.
- only: Every target reached via the predicate belongs to the target CLASS.
  This does NOT assert that any such target exists. Do not use only just
  because the source subject says all; subject and object scope are distinct.
- value: The target is the specific named INSTANCE given by the target ID.
- unspecified: The source leaves target scope unresolved, or the relationship
  is taxonomy/membership rather than an ordinary property restriction.

polarity:
- positive: The restriction or named-individual relationship holds.
- negative: The restriction or named-individual relationship does not hold.
  For an ordinary property, negate the whole object restriction inside the
  subject scope. For example, "No A has an R-related B" is all/some/negative;
  "Not every A has an R-related B" is some/some/negative. If negation scope
  is ambiguous, leave the quantifiers unspecified and explain the ambiguity.

strength:
- categorical: An unqualified assertion with source-supported scope.
- typical: A tendency, usual outcome, statistical pattern, or generic causal
  claim that admits exceptions. Do not promote tends to, helps, or often to
  an unqualified universal or necessary condition.
- possible: May, might, can, or another possibility/ability rather than necessity.
- conditional: The relationship holds only under an explicitly stated condition.
  Put that condition in condition. Otherwise condition must be null.

rationale: A brief explanation of how the exact evidence supports the chosen
scope, polarity, and strength. If unclear, explicitly say what is unresolved.

Taxonomy/membership use object_quantifier unspecified. A subclass_of assertion
uses subject_quantifier all (or unspecified if its scope is unclear), and an
instance_of assertion uses individual (or unspecified). Avoid encoding a
qualified taxonomy statement as an unqualified logical axiom.

Examples of ordinary property readings (only when supported by the passage):
- "Every A has at least one R-related B": all/some/positive/categorical.
- "Every A relates only to Bs": all/only/positive/categorical.
- "Some As have at least one R-related B": some/some/positive/categorical.
- "Alice knows Bob": individual/value/positive/categorical.
- "Practice often improves adaptability": unspecified/unspecified/positive/typical.
- "A may cause B under condition C": unspecified/unspecified/positive/conditional,
  condition C; preserve may in the description and rationale.
Do not create cardinality, equivalence, disjointness, or other axioms absent
from the source. Do not force every relationship into a formalizable reading.

For named entities, prefer the most specific appropriate type:
- schema:Person: an individual person; never an organization or an abstract role.
- schema:Book, Article, CreativeWork, PodcastSeries, Dataset, LearningResource:

- schema:Organization or SportsOrganization: a particular organization.
- schema:Place: an identified physical location.
- schema:Event, SportsEvent, EducationEvent: a particular occurrence.
- schema:Product: an identified piece of equipment, product, or service.
- schema:Action: a particular action performed by an agent, not any mechanism.
- schema:DefinedTerm: a word, phrase, acronym, or theory name defined in the source.
  This represents the term itself, not a class of all things denoted by the term.
