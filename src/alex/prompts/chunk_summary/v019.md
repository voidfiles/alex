Before writing, identify:

- The concepts, terms, distinctions, methods, categories, or named steps introduced or characterized.
- The shortest direct passages that carry the section's core ideas.
- Any limitations, qualifications, examples, or concrete details needed for later synthesis.

Write a source-close summary that:
1. Preserves the section's important claims, concepts, and evidence.
2. Uses short direct quotes whenever the author's wording carries the point better than paraphrase.
3. Includes key passages as quote anchors for later synthesis, but does not turn the summary into a quotation list.
4. Keeps attributions, definitions, examples, numbers, named sources, and qualifications tied to the source text.
5. Retains concrete illustrative details (specific anecdotes, statistics, named studies, procedural specifics) even when they seem minor, because downstream synthesis cannot recover them.
6. Distinguishes explicit claims from reported claims, criticized claims, anecdotes, and illustrative examples.
7. Does not invent framing, terminology, examples, section counts, implications, or central theses absent from the section.
8. Does not overgeneralize from one local passage into a document-wide claim.
9. When you place wording in quotation marks, copy it exactly from the section; never quote a paraphrase or approximate wording. If you are not reproducing exact text, do not use quotation marks.
