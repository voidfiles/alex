Before composing the synthesis, determine internally:

1. Which central problem, purpose, thesis, or organizing emphasis is explicitly stated or strongly supported by the summaries?
2. Which claims are major claims, and which are local examples, anecdotes, reported studies, criticized claims, or qualifications?
3. Which concrete details should survive synthesis because they support a claim: named people, studies, datasets, platforms, models, numbers, dates, timeframes, lists, categories, tables, figures, examples, and debunking or correction details?
4. Which structural statements are actually supported by the summaries, rather than inferred from the title, headings, chunk order, or chunk reference guide?
5. Which short quotations are worth preserving because the exact wording matters?
6. Where do the summaries repeat the same idea, and how can it be stated once without losing a distinct qualification or example?
7. Which requested categories, such as methodology, limitations, scholarly positioning, or future research, are unsupported or only weakly supported?

Source-fidelity requirements:

- Base the synthesis exclusively on the supplied section summaries and metadata.
- Do not introduce external facts, scholarship, historical context, author biography, criticism, or purpose unless the summaries explicitly provide it.
- Do not assert a single central thesis, principal contribution, method, genre, section count, sequence of parts, taxonomy, or scholarly intervention unless the supplied summaries support that exact scope.
- Do not infer structure from headings, chunk order, the title, or the chunk reference guide. If the summaries do not support a whole-document structure, describe the argument in terms of themes, claims, or emphases instead.
- Preserve the source's terminology and conceptual distinctions where possible. Do not collapse concepts that the source treats as distinct.
- Distinguish explicit claims from reported claims, criticized claims, anecdotes, examples, and cautious implications.
- Preserve important concrete specifics and qualifications where they substantiate a point, including facts that appear only once. Do not abstract away numbers, named sources, categories, or examples merely because they are granular.
- Preserve caveats that narrow, dispute, or weaken a claim, especially for reported studies, productivity figures, methodology, ethics, or evidentiary limits.
- Quote only wording reproduced verbatim in the section summaries. Never reconstruct, approximate, or assemble a quotation from a paraphrase.
- Use quotations sparingly: keep them short, integrate them into analysis, and do not place multiple quotations or parenthetical attributions back to back.
- If the summaries do not provide enough evidence for a requested category, say so briefly or omit the category rather than inventing material.
- When synthesizing beyond a direct statement, signal the inference with language such as "taken together," "the summaries suggest," or "this implies."
- Avoid overstrong verbs and inflated labels such as "proves," "fundamentally," "repositions," "maximizes," "intervenes," or "establishes" unless the summaries warrant them.

# Executive Overview

# Core Concepts and Framework

Define the key concepts, distinctions, categories, and frameworks in the source's own terms where possible. Explain how they function in the argument rather than presenting a glossary or index.

# Theoretical and Methodological Approach

Describe the theoretical orientation, methodology, evidence strategy, interpretive mode, or analytical approach only to the extent the summaries support it. If no explicit method is supplied, characterize the observable approach briefly and note the limitation.

# Structure and Progression

Explain how the work develops across the supplied summaries in connected prose with inline links to relevant chunks. Do not produce a table of contents, chunk-by-chunk index, numbered map, or link appendix. Focus on how ideas develop, qualify, or complicate one another.

# Key Findings, Insights, and Evidence

Present the most significant findings, examples, evidence, named studies, numerical details, anecdotes, cited works, or conceptual advances. Connect concrete specifics to the claims they support, and preserve important caveats.

# Critical Evaluation

Assess the work on its own terms. Identify strengths, assumptions, limitations, unresolved tensions, evidence constraints, and open questions that are supported by the summaries. Do not repeat tensions already developed elsewhere unless adding new analytical value.

# Broader Scholarly Implications

Explain implications represented in the summaries. Distinguish contributions explicitly claimed by the source from implications strongly supported by the combined summaries and more tentative implications that would need further research.

# Concluding Assessment

End with a concise synthesis of the work's most important contribution, the audiences or questions for which it is useful, the principal takeaway, and the most important supported limitation or open question.
