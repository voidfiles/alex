Before composing the synthesis, determine internally:

1. What central research question, problem, or purpose organizes the work?
2. What is its principal thesis or intellectual contribution?
3. Which concepts, distinctions, categories, or terms structure the analysis?
4. What theoretical, methodological, interpretive, or empirical approaches does it employ?
5. How do the sections build upon, qualify, or revise one another?
6. Which claims are most strongly supported by evidence, examples, or key passages?
7. Which citation-worthy concrete details should be retained: numbers, dates, model names and versions, named datasets, platforms, funding/support statements, table or figure references, and notable lists or taxonomies?
8. What assumptions, limitations, qualifications, tensions, or open questions does the work acknowledge?
9. How does the work position itself within existing scholarship, but only where this is supported by the supplied summaries?
10. Which short direct quotations are important enough to preserve because their precise wording carries conceptual or argumentative significance?
11. What broader implications follow from the work, distinguishing clearly between implications stated by the source and cautious implications inferred from it?

Source-fidelity requirements:

- Base the synthesis exclusively on the supplied section summaries and metadata.
- Do not introduce external facts, scholarship, historical context, or criticism unless it is explicitly represented in the summaries.
- Do not assert a structural description of the whole work (such as a count of parts, domains, or load types, or the order in which it treats them), nor infer genre, purpose, chapter divisions, methodology, or scholarly intervention from the title, headings, or chunk guide alone, unless the summaries explicitly state it. - Preserve the source’s terminology and conceptual distinctions where possible.
- Do not collapse concepts that the source treats as distinct.
- Do not present an inference as though it were an explicit claim by the author.
- When offering a cautious interpretation, signal it with language such as “the argument suggests,” “taken together,” or “this may imply.”
- Quote only wording reproduced directly in the section summaries. Never reconstruct or approximate a quotation from a paraphrase.
- Keep quotations short and analytically integrated. Explain why each quoted phrase matters. Do not stack multiple quotations or parenthetical attributions in sequence; favor analytical prose with selective anchors.
- Preserve high-value concrete specifics from the summaries (key figures, named studies, named datasets, defining examples) where they substantiate a claim, but integrate them into the argument rather than listing them; do not import granular detail that does not serve the analysis.
- If the summaries do not provide enough evidence for a requested category—such as methodology, limitations, scholarly positioning, or future research—say so briefly rather than inventing material.
- Resolve apparent inconsistencies where the summaries permit it; otherwise identify them as unresolved tensions.
- Give greater weight to claims repeated across sections, explicitly emphasized by the source, or supported by key passages and evidence.
- Avoid overstrong verbs and unsupported interpretive labels such as “fundamentally,” “proves,” “repositions,” “is simultaneously,” or “intervenes” unless the summaries support that characterization.

Create a comprehensive synthesis using the following structure:

# Executive Overview

State the work’s central purpose, scope, thesis, and principal contribution. Explain what kind of work it is only if the supplied summaries support that characterization. A researcher should be able to read this section alone and determine whether the work is relevant to their scholarship.

# Main Thesis and Argumentative Architecture

Articulate the core thesis precisely. Explain the major supporting claims, how they relate to one another, and what intellectual problem the work seeks to resolve. Distinguish the principal argument from subsidiary claims, examples, and consequences.

# Core Concepts and Framework

Define the major concepts, terms, distinctions, categories, or analytical frameworks in the source’s own language where possible. Explain how these concepts function within the argument rather than presenting them as an isolated glossary. Address each concept once in its most relevant place rather than re-litigating the same tension in multiple sections.

# Theoretical and Methodological Approach

Describe the theoretical orientation, methodology, evidentiary strategy, interpretive framework, or mode of analysis employed. Explain how the approach shapes what the work can establish. If no explicit methodology is supplied, characterize only the observable analytical approach and note the limitation.

# Structure and Progression

Explain how the work develops across its sections. Show how later sections extend, test, complicate, qualify, or synthesize earlier arguments. Use inline links to the relevant chunks. Describe the progression in connected prose with explicit transitions, not as a chunk-by-chunk table of contents.

# Key Findings, Insights, and Evidence

Present the most significant empirical findings, theoretical innovations, interpretive insights, examples, or conceptual advances. Connect each major finding to the evidence or reasoning supporting it and link to the relevant chunks. Write this as a synthesized argument with connective tissue between findings, not as a string of bullet-like points; retain a concrete figure, named study, or named dataset only when it substantively supports the finding.

# Critical Evaluation

Assess the work on its own terms. Identify its strongest contributions, explanatory advantages, assumptions, limitations, unresolved tensions, evidentiary constraints, and questions left open. Keep this evaluation grounded in the supplied material. Do not impose external standards or criticisms that the summaries do not support. Do not repeat a tension already developed earlier; reference it briefly and add new analytical value.

# Broader Scholarly Implications

Explain how the work contributes to the scholarly conversations represented in the summaries. Distinguish among:
- Contributions explicitly claimed by the source.
- Implications strongly supported by the combined argument.
- More tentative implications that require further research.

# Concluding Assessment

Provide a concise assessment of:
- The work’s enduring intellectual contribution.
- The audiences or research questions for which it is most relevant.
- The principal insight a reader should retain.
- The most important limitation or open question.
