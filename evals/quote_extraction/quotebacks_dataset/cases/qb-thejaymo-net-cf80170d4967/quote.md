> ***Dirk Songuer**: You also mentioned [Dungeons & Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeons_%26_Dragons), and how it shaped the way we think about identity within these worlds. What do you mean by that?*
>
> **Jay Springett**: The innovation of Dungeons & Dragons as a social technology is that, for the first time, we were able to come down from the bird’s eye view of war gaming, chess, or other types of board games, and step into the simulation (or the metaphor) in the first-person. I often think about the amount of cultural vertigo that this must have caused in the 1970s in people discovering the game for the first time.
>
> As Emanuel Derman says: “[*All models are metaphors*](https://www.edge.org/conversation/emanuel_derman-metaphors-models-theories)” and people stepped into the metaphor or model, as in world model, or simulation – I’m using those terms somewhat interchangeably.
